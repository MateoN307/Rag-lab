# Cornell note-taking method

A page layout and a review routine for lecture notes. This is my personal reference, not the original source.

## Page layout

- **Cue column**: 2.5 inches (6.3 cm) wide, on the left. Keywords and questions go here.
- **Notes area**: 6 inches (15.2 cm) wide, on the right. Main notes during the lecture.
- **Summary area**: 2 inches (5 cm) tall, across the bottom. A short summary written after the lecture.

## Steps

1. **Record**: take notes in the notes area during the lecture. Use short phrases, not full sentences.
2. **Cue**: within 24 hours, write one keyword or question per idea in the cue column.
3. **Reduce**: write a 3 to 5 line summary at the bottom of the page.
4. **Recite**: cover the notes area and say the answers out loud using only the cue column.
5. **Reflect**: write one sentence about what you still do not understand.

## Why it works

Steps 2 and 4 force retrieval. Reading your notes again is easy, but recalling them from a cue is what makes them stick. The reduce step is where you find out whether you understood the lecture.

## My version in Obsidian

- Notes area becomes the body of the note.
- Cue column becomes a `## Cues` heading with bullet points.
- Summary becomes a `## Summary` heading at the end.
- Template: see [[Lecture note template]].

Time guide: 10 minutes for steps 2 and 3, 10 minutes for step 4, once a week per course.
